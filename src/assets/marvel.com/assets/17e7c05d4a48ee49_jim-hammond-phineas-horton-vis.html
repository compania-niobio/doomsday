Jim Hammond and the Legacy of Phineas Horton, Explained

Learn all about the comics history of Jim Hammond and his secret connection to the Vision.

BY Tim Webber

Long before Tony Stark or Reed Richards built mechanical wonders, the Marvel Universe began with a scientist named Phineas T. Horton and his android creation, Jim Hammond. In MARVEL COMICS (1939) #1 by Carl Burgos, Horton unveiled his invention and introduced the Marvel Universe’s first hero, a machine that would become known as the original Human Torch. Although Horton was driven by his obsessive pursuit of fame, the Human Torch forged a heroic legacy with Captain America and the Invaders during World War II. 

Horton and the Human Torch virtually wrote the blueprint for high-tech heroes, playing crucial roles in the Vision’s creation. Marvel Television’s VisionQuest recently paid homage to this history, introducing both Horton and Hammond to the Marvel Cinematic Universe. As they make their on-screen debut, let’s look back at Phineas Horton and Jim Hammond’s history in the comics.

WHO IS PHINEAS HORTON?

Obsessed with fame, Phineas Horton was one of the most brilliant minds of his era. Horton began working for Victor Timely Jr. in 1929, a variant of the time-traveling Kang the Conqueror. One of Horton’s first inventions was the Solar Gem, a powerful device that could absorb sunlight and convert it to energy. He also invented Horton Cells, human-like cells made of polyurethane plastic and carbon. These cells could generate tremendous amounts of power or activate latent superhuman abilities, but they could also burst into flames when exposed to oxygen. 

By combining the Solar Gem with Horton Cells, Horton developed the synthezoids, androids also called synthetic humans. Powered by solar energy and Horton Cells, the synthezoid technology would go on to form the basis of the Human Torch, and eventually, the Vision. Horton developed synthezoid technology with James Bradley, a mutant scientist who eventually joined the X-Men as Doctor Nemesis. However, their partnership shattered when Bradley tried to stop an over-eager Horton from showing off the synthezoid before they figured out how to stop it from catching fire, as detailed in THE MARVELS PROJECT (2009) #1 by Ed Brubaker and Steve Epting. 

THE CREATION OF JIM HAMMOND

When Phineas Horton completed the construction of Jim Hammond, the activation of the heroic synthezoid marked one of the most important moments in the history of the Marvel Universe. However, the public was horrified when Hammond caught fire as Horton unveiled his creation. Horton was then ordered to keep Hammond in an airless chamber, but the Human Torch caught fire again when his cylinder cracked and escaped. 

A mobster named Tony Sardo tried to manipulate the Torch, but he accidentally helped the hero stabilize his powers by exposing him to nitrogen. With Horton’s support, the Human Torch started using his powers to stop crime and began operating as one of the world’s first public heroes. However, the pair split when the Human Torch realized his creator mainly saw him as a vehicle to become rich and famous.

JIM HAMMOND’S EARLY ADVENTURES

As the Human Torch, Jim Hammond quickly established himself as one of the most important heroes of his era. When Namor the Sub-Mariner threatened New York, Hammond faced him in Marvel Comics’ first crossover in MARVEL MYSTERY COMICS (1939) #8 by Bill Everett and Carl Burgos. Hammond also found a sidekick in Toro, a young Inhuman with flame-controlling powers. Despite their initial encounters, the Human Torch, Namor, Captain America, and their respective sidekicks joined forces during World War II as the Invaders. 

The Invaders grew to include several wartime heroes, with Hammond and his teammates playing crucial roles in taking on armies and superhuman threats. After Captain America’s disappearance and the end of the war, Professor Horton built another synthezoid called Adam II in WHAT IF? (1977) #4 by Roy Thomas and Frank Robbins. After the android turned against its creator, Hammond and his squad saved Horton and defeated Adam II.

JIM HAMMOND AND THE VISION

When his powers started dangerously malfunctioning, Jim Hammond deactivated his body and buried himself in the Nevada desert. However, he did not stay buried for long. The Mad Thinker, a villainous scientist, found Hammond’s body and reactivated him to make him fight Johnny Storm, the current Human Torch, in FANTASTIC FOUR ANNUAL (1963) #4 by Stan Lee and Jack Kirby. After Hammond burned himself out again, Ultron found his android body and used it as the basis for his new creation, Vision.

To bring his Vision to life, Ultron kidnapped Phineas Horton, who was living in obscurity, and forced him to reprogram Hammond’s body, as detailed in AVENGERS (1963) #135 by Steve Englehart and George Tuska. Although Horton gave the android a new appearance and powers, he refused to alter his creation’s mind. Ultron decided to kill Horton and completed Hammond’s transformation into the Vision, creating a synthezoid intended to destroy the Avengers.

JIM HAMMOND’S RETURN

Even though Jim Hammond’s body became Vision, the original Human Torch took flight again. Unbeknownst to anyone, Immortus, another of Kang’s time-traveling variants, had secretly created a copy of Hammond’s body as part of a larger scheme. While Horton and Ultron transformed one android body into Vision, the Mad Thinker buried Hammond’s duplicate body in a cemetery. The Scarlet Witch unearthed and reactivated the duplicate Hammond while investigating Vision’s origins in WEST COAST AVENGERS (1985) #50 by John Byrne. 

Reprising his heroic duties as the Human Torch, Hammond briefly joined the Avengers on a few missions. He also reunited with his old wartime allies, Captain America and Namor, joining the Sub-Mariner’s company, Oracle Inc. The original Human Torch also joined a new incarnation of the Invaders and a team of Secret Avengers assembled by Steve Rogers. Although Hammond seemingly died saving the Invaders, the Mad Thinker reactivated him once more in TORCH (2009) #2 by Mike Carey, Alex Ross, and Patrick Berkenkotter. 

HAMMOND AND HORTON’S LEGACY

Despite his heroic history, Jim Hammond struggled to find his place in the modern world. He tried to settle down in a small town, but couldn’t stay out of the action, joining the reformed Invaders and S.H.I.E.L.D. Hammond took point on S.H.I.E.L.D.’s fight against the interdimensional Squadron Supreme and reunited with Captain America to confront Namor, who had reignited his war with the surface world in INVADERS (2019) #1 by Chip Zdarsky, Carlos Magno, and Butch Guice.

Long after his death, Phineas Horton’s legacy still echoed around the Marvel Universe. Vision became a celebrated Avenger, and Horton raised his stepdaughter, Frankie Raye, to be the heroic Nova. Vision ultimately embraced Horton as a father figure and vowed to keep his legacy alive. Although Horton never achieved the notoriety he wanted in life, his creations and his vision for the future still built the foundation for the modern Marvel Universe.
